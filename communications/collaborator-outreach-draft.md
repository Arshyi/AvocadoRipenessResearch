## Suggested subject

Multimodal avocado ripeness study — proposal and request for technical discussion

## Message

Dear colleagues,

I am sharing a proposal titled **“Development of a Multimodal Non-Destructive Avocado Ripeness Characterization System.”** The project would combine controlled RGB imaging, visible/NIR optical measurements, fruit mass, temperature and humidity, CO₂ and exploratory VOC sensing, and matched destructive measurements such as firmness, moisture/dry matter, soluble solids, oil, starch, pigments, and internal condition.

The aim is not simply to build another image classifier. The proposed study treats ripening as a partially observed longitudinal process and asks which low-cost sensing channels add reliable information about internal readiness and remaining useful life. Candidate kinetic and state-space models would be evaluated alongside conventional machine-learning baselines, with all repeated measurements grouped by biological fruit and final testing performed on independent harvest or receipt batches.

The attached package includes:

- an evidence-audited literature and public-dataset review;
- reproducible fruit-grouped RGB baselines and a storage-condition stress test;
- a staged experimental and statistical plan;
- an MVP and research-grade prototype architecture;
- a sourced procurement catalogue; and
- explicit claims, non-claims, risks, and go/no-go criteria.

The public-data analysis found that calibrated peel colour is informative, but its performance falls substantially under storage-condition shift. That result motivates the proposed multimodal and metrology-first design rather than a colour-only deployment claim.

Could we arrange a 30–45 minute discussion to review the scientific scope and feasibility? In particular, I would value your feedback on:

1. the operational definitions of “ready to eat,” “end of useful life,” and the destructive reference protocol;
2. access to independent avocado batches and replicated storage conditions;
3. possible access to NIR equipment, a texture analyser, drying oven and analytical balance, reference ethylene measurement such as an F-900 or validated GC method, and facilities for oil, pigment, starch, and carbohydrate assays; and
4. whether the proposed work could fit an existing laboratory, supervision, student-project, or collaborative research programme.

The immediate decision is whether to proceed with a metrology-first MVP and a two-batch pilot, and which measurements can be supported with available facilities. I would be grateful for any technical corrections, resource suggestions, or recommendations on narrowing the first study.
